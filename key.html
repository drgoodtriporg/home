Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy

Study summary of BrainFutures’ August 2022 elements report. Informational only — not medical advice. Read the report (PDF).

Screening and assessment

  • Physiological and psychological screen.
  • CAPS-5 (PTSD) and MADRS (depression): baseline, then repeat after treatment.
  • Cardiac conditions: major physical contraindications.
  • Drug interactions. Antidepressants often stopped 6–8 weeks before dosing.
  • Trauma assessment here: what may arise later.
  • Common research exclusions: psychosis, and a family history of psychosis.
  • Schizophrenia and bipolar disorder: compound-dependent. See Safety.

Preparation

  • About three 90-minute sessions.
  • Alliance: consciously choose to trust. Thin rapport: consider delaying dosing.
  • Informed consent is ongoing and revocable.
  • Touch agreed beforehand, and revocable in the session.
  • Not a miracle cure. Experience range is wide and unpredictable.
  • Do not ask for a detailed trauma account. Listen if one is offered.
  • Light low-fat breakfast or fast. Comfortable clothes.
  • No driving. Support person for pickup.
  • Beforehand: lower stress; no alcohol or other drugs.

Set and Setting

  • Set: expectations, motivations, intentions, mood.
  • Aim for calm. Postpone if acutely distressed.
  • Setting: living-room-like room, personal items, eyeshades and headphones.
  • Music supports inward focus, emotion, and imagery. Music-evoked emotion is heightened.
  • Secure room, with emergency care reachable.

Medication session

  • Supervised, often 6–8 hours, until effects wear off.
  • Nondirective. Midwifery: reassure when needed; otherwise stay quiet and do not pathologize.
  • Agreed touch, or a grounding presence, if anxiety rises.
  • Blood pressure and heart rate on a schedule.
  • Journal salient moments as effects fade.
  • High doses can bring ego dissolution.

Integration

  • Often starts the morning after. Research: 3+ sessions, about 4–7 hours, over a few weeks.
  • May use ACT or IFS. Tie insights to the diagnosis.
  • On-call therapist. Symptoms can temporarily worsen.
  • Ontological shock and ego dissolution are possible.
  • Avoid major life decisions in the weeks after.

Safety and ethical practice

  • Watch suicidality, especially in early integration.
  • Some complex responses can look like psychosis, mania, or suicidality and may need specialized support “in line with Stanislav Grof’s idea of a spiritual emergency” (Cardenas).
  • Medical complications still need medical care.
  • HPPD (flashbacks or lasting visual distortions): rare, and believed much lower in clinical settings.
  • Two therapists and audio/video recording: research protections, not required in every case. Two therapists raise cost and narrow access.
  • Medical clearance first. Heart rate and blood pressure may rise in session.
  • Sexual misconduct and financial exploitation are named risks in this vulnerable state.

By compound

Ketamine

  • Contraindicated in schizophrenia.
  • Heart failure and hypertension: the primary physical contraindications for ketamine.

MDMA

  • Raises heart rate and blood pressure. Phase 2 trials excluded cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease.
  • May worsen bipolar disorder and schizophrenia.

Psilocybin

  • Psychotic-spectrum illness and a family history are common exclusions.
  • Bipolar depression: risk not well understood. Use caution.
